How to Get a Lower Mortgage Rate: 5 Practical Steps

Updated August 9, 20268 min readBy NiONlite LLC

No strategy can guarantee the market's lowest mortgage rate, but you can improve the quality of the offers you receive. Prepare your credit, compare standardized Loan Estimates, evaluate the full cost of points and fees, and make a deliberate rate-lock decision.

1

Maximize Your Credit Score

Lenders generally use credit scores and credit-report information to decide whether you qualify and what rate to offer. Higher scores often provide access to more affordable offers, but income, debt, savings, loan type, property, and lender rules also matter.

Review your credit reports early enough to dispute errors before applying. Continue paying bills on time, avoid taking on unnecessary new debt, and ask a qualified housing counselor or financial professional for advice tailored to your circumstances.

The CFPB says the lowest rates generally go to borrowers with credit scores in the mid-to-high 700s or above, but there are no firm rules that guarantee a particular rate at a particular score.

2

Compare Multiple Lenders on the Same Terms

The CFPB recommends comparing at least three loan offers. Include a mix of lenders that fit your situation, such as banks, credit unions, online lenders, and lenders experienced with VA, FHA, USDA, jumbo, or first-time-buyer programs.

Ask for the same loan type, term, down payment, point structure, and lock period. Then compare the standardized Loan Estimates rather than relying only on website advertisements.

  • Compare interest rate, APR, points, and lender credits
  • Review origination charges and total cash to close
  • Check whether the quoted rate is locked and until when
  • Ask how changed assumptions would affect the offer
3

Choose a Down Payment by Total Cost

Down payment size can affect the rate, loan amount, mortgage insurance, cash reserves, and monthly payment. Conventional borrowers putting down less than 20% are often required to carry mortgage insurance, but the right choice depends on the entire loan and your available savings.

Ask lenders to show multiple down-payment scenarios and compare total costs. Eligible borrowers should also ask about government-backed options; VA, FHA, and USDA loans follow different rules and can be competitive in the right situation.

4

Understand the Rate Lock Before You Commit

An unlocked rate can change. A rate lock generally protects the quoted rate for a stated period as long as the loan closes on time and the application assumptions do not change.

Ask when the lock starts, when it expires, whether there is a fee, what an extension costs, and which changes could invalidate the lock. The right moment depends on your closing timeline and risk tolerance; predicting short-term market movement is not reliable.

A published-rate alert in CUB can help you notice lender movement, but it is not a personalized quote or lock. Confirm the current offer and lock terms directly with the lender.

5

Consider Buying Points (Strategically)

One discount point equals 1% of the loan amount, but the resulting rate reduction is not fixed. It depends on the lender, loan, and market. Points exchange more money at closing for a lower rate; lender credits generally do the reverse.

Ask each lender for comparable options with and without points. Estimate the break-even period by dividing the upfront point cost by the monthly payment reduction, then consider how long you realistically expect to keep the loan.

  • Calculate your break-even point before buying down
  • Compare the same number of points across lenders
  • Consider the chance of selling or refinancing before break-even
  • Ask lenders for both a "with points" and "no points" quote to compare

The Bottom Line

A strong mortgage-shopping process is more reliable than chasing a headline rate. Prepare your credit, compare at least three equivalent offers, understand total cash and long-term cost, and document your lock terms before proceeding.
